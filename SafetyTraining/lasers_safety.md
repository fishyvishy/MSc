## Laser Basics 
- Laser = Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation
- Laser Device: Produces coherent, directional beam of monochromatic light 
- Laserc Classification number **increases** with beam hazard 
- embedded laser: enclosed laser that lowers hazard
- Diffuse Reflection: beam reflected in many directions, changes spatial distribution
- Specular reflection: mirror-like reflection -> no change in beam spatial distribution

- Maximum Permissible Expose (MPE): max unprotected exposure level without biological changes to eye or skin 
- Nominal Hazard Zone (NHZ): space around laser where hazard exceeds MPE 
- Aversion Response: closing of eyelid or movement of head to avoid exposure. Occurs within 0.25 seconds. 


## Laser Beam Creation
1. Active medium: + wavelength determines output 
2. Excitation Mechanism: input energy to create laser
3. Optical Resonator: mirrors facing each other that reflect light into beam 

## Laser Categories 
1. Solid-State: uses optically transparent materials, like glass or crystal./ Operate in continuous wave (CW) or pulsed
2. Semi Conductor (diode) lasers
Most common laser. 3 families 
   - GaAIAs (gallium/aluminum/arsenide) 750nm-950nm. Used in CD players
   - inGaASP (indium/gallium/arsenide/phosphide) 1100nm-1650nm. Optical telecom
   - AlGalnP (aluminum/gallium/arsenide/phosphide). Visible spectrum, commonly red. Used in gas sensors. 


1. Liquid (dye) Lasers: more versatile, but liquids are more hazardous 
2. Gas lasers


## Laser Classifications
1. Class 1: incapable of hurting you. Commonplace. Laser printers/CD players 
2. Class 1M: safe for naked eye, but if magnifying optics used, then a little dangerous. M class modifier usually applies to expanded or diverging beams
3. Class 1C: new (2018). Used directly on skin but not eyes. May exceed MPE. Used in hair removals 

1. Class 2: visible wavelength. eyes protected by aversion response. Max 1mW CW output. Laser pointers/barcode scanners. 
2. Class 2M ...: 

1. Class 3R (replaced 3A): "Reduced Requirements". Safe if handled well, and viewing restricted. Severe injuries rare, but possible. Not diffuse reflection hazard. Max CW power <= 5mW. (inside CD-DVD players)
2. Class 3b: Direct and specular reflection may be hazardous. Protective yewear normally required for direct viewing.
Needs key switch and safety interlock 
CW power <= 500mW

1. Class 4
Highest and most dangerous class. Hazardous to eye and skin from direct, specular, and diffuse reflections. No limits on power >= 500mW. Fire hazard. Plasma burns possible. Laser-generated air contaminants (LGACs) may be released. 


Class 4 and 3B can be turned into class 1 if beam mechanisms are completely enclosed in protective containment. 


## Laser Hazards 
1. Beam Exposure (radiation hazards) eye/skin
2. Associated Hazards (non-beam)

### Eye Hazards 
- Retina damage can heal. Occur in visible light range (400nm to 1400 nm). UVA and UVB/UVC (lower/higher wavelengths) are absorbed by cornea/aqueous humour. UV damage occurs at corena
- Retina lacks pain sensors. 
- Fovea damage longer-lasting. 
- Cornea is first point of contact
- Eye magnifies 10000x

Eye Injury signs 
- sudden apearance of floaters
- headaches
- feeling sand in eyes 
- visual distortions

## Laser Accidents
- unexpected exposure during alignment
- not using appropriate eye protection  

- Laser device must be clearly visible. Don't clutter the optical bench. 
- Use Laser controlled area 
    - well defined path
    - limited access
    - posted warning signs

- no jewelry, exposed clothing, or **makeup**
- keep beam height away from eye level


- laser warning signs need to describe laser type, warning symbol, max output power, max pulse energy, class, ranges


## Beam power 
- Optical Density. Eyeware rated for OD = base-10 log of attenuation factor by which optical filter reduces beam power. 
    - OD 3 reduces beam power by factor of 1000 
    - measures absorption (or transmission) of a material 
    - OD is wavelength dependent. 

Laser Intensity = I = Power/Aperature 

$OD = \log_{10}(1/T_\lambda) = \log_{10}(I/MPE_\lambda)$

Where 
$T_\lambda$ = transmittance 
$I$ = potential laser exposure (irradiance/intensity)
$MPE_\lambda$ = maximum permissible exposure given wavelength

OD is rounded up. 


- PPE designed for one wavelength inadequate for others. Dangerous with tunable lasers
- Alternative, use cameras to prevent exposure
